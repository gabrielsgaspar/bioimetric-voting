\section{Empirical Design}
\label{sec:empirical-design}

\textbf{Staggered rollout.} The empirical design uses the staggered rollout of biometric voter registration across Brazilian municipalities. Municipalities entered BVR in different election years, while a large set of municipalities remained never treated through the end of the sample. This timing creates a panel design in which each treated cohort can be compared to municipalities that had not entered the biometric regime, with municipality histories anchoring the pre-treatment path and election-year comparisons absorbing national shocks common to all municipalities in a given election cycle.

The main registry and composition figures use the Callaway--Sant'Anna group-time difference-in-differences estimator \citep{Callaway2020DifferenDifferenWith}. For treatment cohort $g$ and election year $t$, the primitive estimand is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:cs-att}
ATT(g,t)=\mathbb{E}\left[Y_t(g)-Y_t(0)\mid G=g\right],
\end{equation}
where $G$ is the first treatment year and $Y_t(0)$ is the no-BVR potential outcome. I aggregate these group-time effects by event time $k=t-g$, using event time $-2$ as the reference period. The displayed window is eight years before and after adoption, so $k \in \{-8,-6,-4,-2,0,2,4,6,8\}$. The reference period is included in the plots at zero to make the normalization explicit. Standard errors are clustered at the municipality level, and the preferred implementation uses never-treated municipalities as the control group.

\textbf{Estimator choice.} I use the Callaway--Sant'Anna estimator as the main estimator because the treatment is staggered and treatment effects are likely to vary across adoption cohorts and over event time. A conventional dynamic TWFE event study is useful as a transparent diagnostic, but under staggered adoption it can combine treatment effects with non-convex weights when effects are heterogeneous. The group-time estimator instead estimates cohort-specific treatment effects against a clean comparison group and then aggregates them into the common event-time profile shown in the paper. Appendix~\ref{app:alternative-estimators} compares the main Callaway--Sant'Anna path to dynamic TWFE, Borusyak--Jaravel--Spiess, and de Chaisemartin--D'Haultfoeuille alternatives for the core registry outcomes. The estimated trajectories are qualitatively similar across estimators.

\textbf{Strict and hybrid BVR.} The preferred treatment contrast is exposure to full biometric registration, which corresponds to municipalities that entered the strict BVR regime. Hybrid implementation is a lower-intensity form of exposure: some voters could update biometric records before full enforcement, but the municipality had not fully transitioned into the strict regime. For this reason, the main estimates use the strict-BVR treatment timing and exclude municipalities ever flagged as hybrid, leaving 2{,}480 treated municipalities and 1{,}245 never-treated controls in the registry-size specification. Appendix~\ref{app:including-hybrid} repeats the exercise with strict and hybrid municipalities included in the treatment definition. The hybrid-inclusive estimates preserve the sign and shape of the results but are attenuated on impact: the registry-size effect falls from about $-0.117$ log points in the strict sample to about $-0.068$ log points when hybrid municipalities are included, while the low-education and high-education effects fall from about 8.9 percentage points to about 5.1 percentage points.

\textbf{Outcomes.} The first set of outcomes measures the electorate itself: log registered voters, the share of registered voters with low education, and the share with high education. These are the outcomes in Section~\ref{sec:electorate-size} and Section~\ref{sec:electorate-composition}. The decomposition in Section~\ref{sec:decomposition} then separates the registry contraction into exit from the voter roll and education re-labeling. Downstream analyses apply the same timing logic to turnout, blank and null voting, presidential vote shares, municipal fiscal outcomes, health outcomes, and survey-based measures of trust and electoral legitimacy where the data support the design.

\textbf{Diagnostics and robustness.} Several checks discipline the interpretation of the event studies. The figures show pre-treatment coefficients relative to event time $-2$, so visible pre-trends would be evidence against the design. Appendix~\ref{app:alternative-estimators} checks that the core registry results are not driven by estimator-specific features of the Callaway--Sant'Anna aggregation. Appendix~\ref{app:including-hybrid} shows that the same patterns appear when hybrid municipalities are included, with smaller magnitudes consistent with a lower-intensity treatment. Appendix~\ref{app:honestdid} applies HonestDiD-style sensitivity tests to the pre-trend restrictions, and Appendix~\ref{app:additional-robustness} reports state-by-year fixed effects, within-state support restrictions, permutation tests, negative-weight diagnostics, and cohort-balance checks. Across these exercises, the central registry and composition results remain stable.
