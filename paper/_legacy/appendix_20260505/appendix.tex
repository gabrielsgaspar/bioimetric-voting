\section{Excluding Hybrid Municipalities}
\label{app:exclude-hybrid}

This appendix reruns the dynamic TWFE event studies after excluding every municipality ever flagged as \textit{Híbrido} in the official 2018 municipal biometric-status source. That restriction removes 1,533 municipalities from the analysis sample and leaves 4,038 municipalities in the panel, of which 2,793 are treated and 1,245 remain never treated. The hybrid status source is municipality-specific but only observed for 2018, so this exercise should be read as a conservative robustness check that removes municipalities with documented partial implementation from the estimating sample.

The resulting estimates are close to the baseline results in the main text. For the size of the electorate, the impact effect becomes slightly larger in absolute value, moving from about $-0.115$ log points in the full sample to $-0.117$ log points in the non-hybrid sample, and the subsequent recovery remains gradual. For electorate composition, the impact estimates are almost unchanged: the low-education share still falls by about 8.9 percentage points and the high-education share still rises by about 9.0 percentage points. The main difference is in the farthest pre-treatment lead, which is somewhat farther from zero for the education-share outcomes once hybrid municipalities are removed. That pattern suggests that the core post-treatment findings are not being driven by hybrid jurisdictions, while also reinforcing the need to interpret the longest event-time horizons cautiously.

\section{Alternative Estimator Comparisons}
\label{app:alternative-estimators}

Because biometric registration rolls out at different times across municipalities, the TWFE event studies in the main text are only one way to summarize the dynamics. This appendix compares those baseline plots to alternative staggered-adoption estimators \citep{Callaway2020DifferenDifferenWith,Abraham2018EstimatiDynamicTreatmen,Borusyak2024RevisitiEventStudy}. Across outcomes, the broad pattern is stable: BVR produces an immediate contraction in the registry and a persistent shift away from lower-education registrants and toward higher-education registrants. The main difference across estimators is precision at the longest horizons, where support becomes thin.
