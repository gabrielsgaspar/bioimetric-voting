## Title

**In Technology We Trust? Biometric Voter Registration in Brazil, Electoral Access, and Democratic Attitudes**

## Core Framing

The paper studies the tradeoff between electoral integrity and electoral access. Brazil's biometric voter registration program was introduced to secure voter identification and modernize election administration, but reforms of this kind can also change the burdens of registration and participation. The project's central question is whether a security-oriented electoral reform can also alter political inclusion, participation, and citizens' views of democratic institutions.

## Main Questions

1. Did BVR reduce the size of the electorate in the short run, and did the registry recover?
2. Did BVR change the composition of the electorate, especially by education and gender?
3. Is BVR exposure associated with trust in institutions and support for democracy?
4. Are these associations concentrated in specific respondent groups?
5. Did BVR feed later skepticism about electoral integrity or ballot secrecy?

## Empirical Blocks

- Administrative municipality-year analysis using TSE data.
- LAPOP repeated cross-sections for trust and democracy.
- Later-wave backlash and integrity analysis using LAPOP.

## Working Principles

- Preserve raw-data integrity and document provenance.
- Keep raw, interim, and clean data products distinct.
- Treat treatment timing, municipality harmonization, and hybrid implementation as substantive design choices.
- Separate descriptive evidence from stronger causal claims.
- Keep paper claims traceable to code, generated tables, and generated figures.
